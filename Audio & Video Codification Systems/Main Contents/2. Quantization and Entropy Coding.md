## Differential PCM
This PCM uses THE DIFFERENCE between consecutive samples rather than their volume at that point. It removes redundancy and also correlates the different consecutive sounds

```ad-caution
For this use of correlation, **this doesn't work well on noise**
```

```ad-important
-80dB is the lowest volume achieved with 16 bits
```

Common DPCM also incorporate short term prediction process

```ad-important
**Everytime quantization is done, there is some noise to it, that is of course reduced with more bits**.
- With a speaker, if we increase the volume, there's the signal with increased volume and *also an increased noise*
- That means that some other signals will be under the volume of the *noise*
- In CD quality, we can put up to 80 dB to avoid noise, as quantization noise appears after it given CD quality bit rate
- Take a sound of a plane and increase the speaker to 120, then the noise is maybe 60 db, and the needle dropping is less than 60 dB

Quantization noise is always proportional to the signal volume
```

# Entropy coding
![[2-Quantization-and -entropy-coding.pdf#page=22&rect=1,2,719,360|2-Quantization-and -entropy-coding, p.22]]
By using the least optimized coding, we get the entropy and this will tell us the smallest amount of bits necessary for encoding AND THE MEASURE OF UNCERTAINTY OF THE VOLUME R.V, which in this case is 2 with entropy code and 3 with uniform code (000, 001, 010, 011, 101, 110, 110)

Statistical entropy alone does not provide a good measure of comprehensibility in the case of audio coding. Quantization noise, masking thresholds, and tone- and noise masking effects must be accounted for - *The best entropy coding ends up being Huffman codes*

```ad-summary
Based on the pdf, **we store indexes rather than the samples**. HUffman codes do not code the samples, they store indexes were then, compression is applied

(WE ENCODE THE VOLUME VALUES (THAT ARE TREATED AS SYMBOLS THAT CAN BE REPEATED) RATHER THAN ALL THE SAMPLES, WHOSE REPETITION WOULD BE ENCODED AS DIFFERENT SYMBOLS)

On something like white noise, where the PDF is a straight line over the whole range, no compression can be done since the volume applies the same on the entire range 
Souds that work well for coding are those that oscillate a lot around zero, like vowels, where the first "hit" is followed by small oscillations around zero.

THE PDF CAN BE NON SYMMETRIC.
- Take a sound decreasing in volume, the peaks start going down, so maybe the first peak to 1 will be followed by -0.8 instead
```
