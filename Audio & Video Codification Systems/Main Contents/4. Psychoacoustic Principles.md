The basilar membrane motivates non linear analysis of the **frequency bands**, therefore, this band is a *filter bank* (of bandpass filters)
- The responses are not symmetric nor linear


# Critical bands and Bark scale
In this scale, we have more resolution in the low frequencies compared to the high, therefore, we find many more frequency samples for low frequencies. **The membrane works this way**
![[4-Psychoacoustic-principles.pdf#page=7&rect=96,54,853,403|4-Psychoacoustic-principles, p.7]]
	Lower frequencies are represented by *narrow bands*, so MANY, as to allocate for all the frequencies in each bank
	Higher frequencies are represented by *wide bands*, but less, as to allocate for the higher frequencies (which have a poor resolution) in a more extended range

A common approximation, as shown in the graph, is using **25 filters** - USED FOR QUANTIZATION AND SUCH, DOESN'T MEAN ALL THE FREQUENCIES IN THAT BAND ARE HEARD AS JUST ONE. Perceptually though, they're grouped like that for quantization and since it models the basilar membrane behavior.

It just refers to the different bandpasses in the auditory systems, not that we can only hear the 25 frequencies in the bark scale. Even within a bandpass, that bandpass still encompasses many frequencies, which we can hear, but are within one filter bank

# Time (Non-simultaneous) masking
It refers to how a sound can be masked before or after another sound is played

Postmasking: Can hide artifacts shortly after, longer than premasking

# Spatial psychoacoustics
## Spatial hearing: Binaural cues
**Interaural Time Difference (ITD):** Different arrival time at each ear due to the path lengths
	In codification, it is likely that a sound from the right will be slightly different on the left ear, thus coding on the left ear can be done differently.

**Interaural Level Difference (ILD)**: Different sound pressure levels due to head shadowing, especially at high frequencies (one ear hears louder than the other due to speaker position)
	In codification, a different level MIGHT result in less bits being needed for codification

## Spatial coherence
**Interaural Coherence (IC)**: Degree of similarity between left and right signals *at the ears*
	In coding, we can avoid coding signals twice (as they may sound the same between signals)
**Inter-channel Coherence**: Degree of similarity between left and right signals

Coherence is much better for low frequencies than high frequencies, as the latter present a faster period, and thus they change more between both of the ears:
![[4-Psychoacoustic-principles.pdf#page=19&rect=546,203,914,412|4-Psychoacoustic-principles, p.19]]
	Low frequencies can be encoded as a single channel due to their similarity
